# Seed 02 — Algeria: injection molders, mold makers, machine dealers and copying risk (supply side, as of 2026-10-04)

**Context.** Who already makes plastic parts and molds in Algeria, who sells and services machines, where capacity is crowded or thin, and what is known about copying. Seed document for a multi-agent simulation of a one-press (100–120 t) B2B entrant.

## 1. Sector size and clusters

- The sector has about 1,000 mostly SME firms, roughly 40% injection, 40% packaging/plasticulture, 20% extrusion. [WEAK SIGNAL · Plasteurope; F-IND §5] Another source counts 580 plastics and rubber firms, dominated by K-Plast and STPM, and calls industrial subcontracting "underdeveloped". [WEAK SIGNAL · Algérie360; F-IND §5]
- Kompass lists only 12 injection molders (Algiers 4, Blida 2, Boumerdès 2, Oran 2, El Oued 1, Tlemcen 1) and 9 mold companies nationally, far below the sector size. [STRONG SIGNAL · Kompass DZ; P1-SUP §3]
- AAPI registered 675 plastics-sector investment projects worth DZD 138.7 bn between Feb 2022 and Feb 2026 (383 new, 292 extensions; 239 in 2025; 51 in operation, 242 advanced; 15,150 jobs expected). [VERIFIED 2026-03-02 · APS, Algérie Eco; F-IND §4]
- Dense clusters: East Algiers–Boumerdès (Rouiba, Oued Smar, Kouba, Dar El Beida, Tassala El Merdja, Ouled Moussa, Khemis El Khechna, Bordj Menaiel), Sétif–El Eulma, Bordj Bou Arréridj (BBA). Blida is a secondary packaging cluster. Tissemsilt hosts the state auto plant GPI. [STRONG SIGNAL · P1-SUP §1]
- Oran shows only commodity molders on record (e.g. Etoile Plastique, Es Senia: injected packaging, drums, jerrycans, furniture; Plast Windows Oran: plastic hardware). [STRONG SIGNAL · El Mouchir; P1-SUP §1] Names of the two Kompass-listed Oran molders are not visible. [UNKNOWN]
- Constantine, Annaba, Skikda, Batna, Tizi Ouzou, Béjaïa and Sidi Bel Abbès showed almost no supply-side firms beyond single names. [WEAK SIGNAL · P1-SUP §1]

## 5. Crowded vs thin segments

- Crowded on record: preforms and caps (SGT, PTD, BENAL Khenchela), packaging/thermoforming/IML, household goods (chairs, buckets, crates), pipes and fittings, large logistics parts. [STRONG SIGNAL · P1-SUP §3; F-IND §5]
- Household plastics (Maghreb-wide): basic models saturated, price competition against Chinese imports and counterfeits; informal under-priced imports ~15% of supply. [WEAK SIGNAL · La Vie Éco; F-IND §5]
- Thin on record: small technical, electrical and industrial parts made to customer drawings; mold repair outside Algiers and Sétif; machine service for brands other than Yizumi. [WEAK SIGNAL · P1-SUP §3]

## 6. Copying and imitation

- In 2011 the customs DG said 60% of goods imported for resale were counterfeit. [WEAK SIGNAL · Afrik.com; P1-SUP §5]
- A Commerce Ministry survey (c. 2011–13) found 40.98% of counterfeits were made locally, 41.34% imported, 18% unknown; auto spare parts were among the most counterfeited goods. [WEAK SIGNAL · Algérie360, Afrik.com; P1-SUP §5]
- 700,000 counterfeit articles were seized in 2011 (+845%). [WEAK SIGNAL · Echorouk; P1-SUP §5]
- Several firms combine mold making and molding (MIRAF, SNK, ENPC/SIPLAST+ALMOULES, FMPI), and some advertise making parts "from models" or reverse engineering (Cumnewtec, Precision Industry, CDTA). [STRONG SIGNAL · P1-SUP §5]
- No forum, social-media or court case of an Algerian molder copying a specific customer's or competitor's part was found. [UNKNOWN · P1-SUP §5]
- Algeria's IP environment ranks among the weakest internationally (see seed 07). [VERIFIED 2026 · US Chamber IP Index; P5]

## Assumptions (not facts)

- Threat ranking for a 100–120 t technical-parts shop near Algiers: (1) SNK Plastic (same model, nearby, sees customers' molds); (2) informal Ouedkniss molders and moulistes (cheap, fast, imitative); (3) MIRAF; (4) captive OEM shops that could in-source a profitable part. [ASSUMPTION · P1-SUP §2]
- Copying risk is highest for the entrant's own catalogue products (standard clips, plugs, spacers) and lower for parts made to one customer's drawing under that customer's brand. [ASSUMPTION · P1-SUP §5]
- Main copying channels: giving parts or molds to a molder that also makes molds; mold makers reusing designs; employees moving between nearby firms. [ASSUMPTION · P1-SUP §5]
- East Algiers–Boumerdès is the best area for support (molds, repairs, dealers) and the worst for exposure to capable copiers; Sétif/El Eulma and BBA big players are captive; Oran has thin documented capacity (directories under-count). [ASSUMPTION · P1-SUP §1]
- New capital (AAPI's 675 projects, large-tonnage machines) flows mostly into commodity and packaging parts. [ASSUMPTION · P1-SUP §3]
- Local mold makers are best for simple single-cavity molds, repairs, modifications and inserts; if the 90–120 day figure applies they are not faster than China. [ASSUMPTION · F-MOLD §1]
- Algerian subcontract pricing is quoted per piece including material, not as an hourly rate. [ASSUMPTION · F-COST §2]
- GPI may need Tier-2 subcontractors and mold makers. [ASSUMPTION · F-IND §5]
- Yizumi via AFC is the lowest-risk first-machine route for local service; buying another brand direct requires lining up a freelance technician first. [ASSUMPTION · P1-SUP §4]
