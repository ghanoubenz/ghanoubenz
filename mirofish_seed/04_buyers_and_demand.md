# Seed 04 — Algeria: buyers and demand for small B2B plastic parts, by sector (as of 2026-10-04)

**Context.** Which Algerian buyers could purchase small injection-molded parts (≤120 t press), what they buy today, and what prices are visible. **Key fact for the simulation: apart from one founder anecdote, no named Algerian buyer with a documented sourcing problem was found in any sector.** Seed document for a multi-agent simulation.

## Assumptions (not facts)

- The shampoo maker's problem is specific, not national: a dispensing closure (flip-top/disc-top) matched to one bottle neck, needing a brand-funded mold (US$2k–15k, 6–10 weeks) and short runs (10–50k per colour) that local molders refuse; the likely sentence heard was "we don't have a mold for that; a mold costs X and the minimum is Y". [ASSUMPTION · P1-PC §1; P2-CL §1]
- Landed cost of imported caps = FOB × 237 DZD/USD at official FX, × 428 at parallel FX (freight 1.2 × duties 1.35 × clearing 1.1). Local unit cost of a flip-top ≈2.2–2.75 DZD vs ≈4.7–5.9 DZD landed at official FX, so local margin exists mainly for parallel-rate buyers, sub-10k runs, urgent supply, or brand-funded molds. [ASSUMPTION · P2-CL §3]
- The prior project estimate of 8–25 DZD per flip-top is unsourced. [ASSUMPTION · P1-PC §5]
- Small brands buy bottle and closure as a set from one trader or blower, so a closure entrant must sell through or alongside blowers. [ASSUMPTION · P1-PC §3]
- Multinationals buy closures through global contracts and have the lowest pain. [ASSUMPTION · P2-CL §4]
- Construction consumables retail at 3–7× the landed import floor; the local molder competes with the importer's and distributor's margin, not the Chinese factory. [ASSUMPTION · P2-JC §3]
- Joinery accessories split into many system-specific SKUs; universal consumables (packers, drainage caps, fly-screen corners) avoid this. [ASSUMPTION · P2-JC §1]
- Irrigation distributor buying peaks before autumn transplanting and potato planting; stock must be built in the off-season. Furniture peaks before the September school year. [ASSUMPTION · P2-IF §3]
- The poultry nipple is the only part with sourced FOB headroom (local body ≈19 DZD vs landed 56–73 DZD at official FX). [ASSUMPTION · P2-IF §4]
- The car-aftermarket shortage probably covers plastic clips and fasteners. [ASSUMPTION · P2-OS §1]
- "Plastic spares for state energy companies" (reverse-engineer, prototype, then mold repeat parts) is the best pain-backed lane; supplier registration is the gate. [ASSUMPTION · P2-OS §1]
- Verdicts used by the project: KILL — beverage caps, pumps/triggers, automotive OEM (near term), emitters, medical, stationery, cable ties, glands, junction boxes as a lead. GO-candidate/INVESTIGATE — stock flip-top/disc-top 24/410 and 28/410, rebar spacers, tile clips, glazing packers, drip-tape fittings, poultry nipple parts, furniture caps and glides, anchors, Sonatrach spares service. All GO-candidates rest on price gaps, not demonstrated pain. [ASSUMPTION · P1-PC §6; P2-JC §5; P2-IF §6; P2-OS §9]
