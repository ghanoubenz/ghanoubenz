# Seed 05 — Algeria: how industrial buyers decide, pay, switch and fund tooling (as of 2026-10-04)

**Context.** Behavioural evidence on Algerian B2B buyers of plastic components: buying-centre structure, payment instruments and risks, switching triggers, mold funding and trust. **Hard evidence exists for payment instruments and policy shocks; almost none exists for sample approval, switching, trust or tooling norms.** Seed document for a multi-agent simulation.

**Evidence tags.** `[VERIFIED date · source]` = official text or several consistent sources, seen in snippets (no page read in full). `[STRONG SIGNAL]`, `[WEAK SIGNAL]`, `[UNKNOWN]` as usual; `[ASSUMPTION]` only in the last section. Source notes: `Phase 2-8/p3_buyer_behavior.md` (P3), `data/phase3_behavior_signals.csv`, feasibility `cost_inputs_market_prices.md` (F-COST), `molds_sourcing_shipping.md` (F-MOLD), `p2_closures.md` (P2-CL), `p2_joinery_construction.md` (P2-JC). Import controls, PPI, cabas and domiciliation rules are in seed 01.

## 1. Who decides

- In Algerian family SMEs, centralised decision-making by the owner-founder is a defining trait. [STRONG SIGNAL · Univ. Béjaïa research; P3 §1]
- The owner-manager's profile is a main explanatory factor of differences in management practice. [WEAK SIGNAL · Revue univ-Biskra (HR, not purchasing); P3 §1]
- Larger groups have formal purchasing: Univers Détergent recruits a junior "acheteur technique" to analyse needs with departments, select and negotiate suppliers, follow delivery and quality; Biaxial Films Packaging (Sétif), MCE (Khemis El Khechna) and Vital Care recruit buyers; Elite Procurement Services sources local and international suppliers. [VERIFIED 2026 · Emploitic job ads; P3 §1]
- Cosider Canalisations used AHP multi-criteria supplier scoring on quality, delivery time, cost and financial health. [STRONG SIGNAL · ASJP article; P3 §1] A Sonelgaz (east) case study applies the same method. [WEAK SIGNAL · Univ. M'sila; P3 §1]
- Standard supplier file for tenders and consultations: RC extract, NIF, NIS, article d'imposition, extrait de rôle, current CNAS and CASNOS certificates. [VERIFIED 2022 · Safqatic consultation file; P3 §1]
- Who physically approves samples, how many signatures a new supplier needs, and the role of the accountant/DAF in family SMEs are not documented. [UNKNOWN · P3 §1]
- Large buyers mold in-house: Condor, Brandt, SIPLAST; Venus reportedly makes its own packaging. [VERIFIED / WEAK SIGNAL · see seed 02; P3 §1]

## 2. How they pay

- Instruments in use: company cheques, certified bank cheques, SWIFT transfers, bills of exchange (traite), promissory notes, documentary collection and documentary credit. [STRONG SIGNAL · Crédit Agricole International country page; P3 §2]
- Distribution chain: small retailers pay distributors in cash; distributors deposit cash and pay manufacturers/importers by cheque or transfer. [STRONG SIGNAL · TSA Dec 2025; P3 §2]
- The Bank of Algeria banned cash deposits into commercial accounts from 23 Dec 2025; a note of 4 Jan 2026 allowed funding by scriptural means or cash in line with turnover and client profile. [VERIFIED 2025-12-22 / 2026-01-04 · APS, TSA; P3 §2] Other outlets describe a one-month transition then a renewed ban from end-Jan 2026; the regime from Feb 2026 is not confirmed. [WEAK SIGNAL / UNKNOWN · Algérie Eco, ObservAlgérie; P3 §2]
- The governor was replaced by an interim at the time of the reversal. [STRONG SIGNAL · TSA; P3 §2]
- Fewer than 80,000 card terminals served >2 million merchants up to end-April 2025. [STRONG SIGNAL · Algérie Eco/TSA; P3 §2]
- Finance Law 2025 art. 207 bans cash for property, dealer vehicle sales, yachts and mandatory insurance, not ordinary B2B goods. [VERIFIED 2024-12-31 · Algérie Eco, TSA; P3 §2]
- A mandatory scriptural-payment threshold of 500,000 DZD was reported (probably c. 2010–11); the current threshold is not confirmed. [WEAK SIGNAL / UNKNOWN · Algérie360; P3 §2]
- Bounced cheques are a délit under Penal Code arts. 374–375; BoA Regulation 08-01 requires banks to consult the Centrale des impayés before issuing chequebooks, declare incidents within 4 working days, and allows one free regularisation per year within 10 days, otherwise a 5-year cheque ban. [STRONG SIGNAL · university theses, BoA PDF snippet; P3 §2]
- The Centrale des impayés recorded 64,699 cheque incidents worth 99.4 bn DZD at end-2016, sharply up on 2015. [STRONG SIGNAL · Algérie360; P3 §2] Users see cheques as unsecured; few institutions use the BoA security device. [STRONG SIGNAL · Algérie360, undated; P3 §2]
- No Algerian decriminalisation of bounced cheques was found (Tunisia and Morocco reformed in 2024–26). [UNKNOWN · P3 §2]
- Public payers are slow: contractors claimed 130 bn DZD of unpaid public receivables (2017); ~35,000 BTP firms still awaited dues despite a >60 bn DZD injection. [STRONG SIGNAL · Le360 2017, Echorouk; P3 §2]
- ENIEM (public appliance maker): raw-material stockouts, no bank credit for supplies, full stop in 2023, unpaid wages Oct 2023–Jan 2024, ~5 bn DZD debt. [VERIFIED 2023–24 · Algérie Eco, TSA; P3 §2]
- Factoring by banks was regulated only by BoA Regulation 26-03 of 13 Aug 2026 (JO no. 67); Afreximbank/FCI ran a factoring workshop in Algiers in Oct 2025. [STRONG SIGNAL · Algérie Eco, AllAfrica; P3 §2]
- Private credit stays below 25% of GDP. [WEAK SIGNAL · Algérie Eco, undated; P3 §2]
- Half of operators' revenue is said to come from the informal economy; sales without invoices ~155 bn DZD over three years; wholesalers refuse invoices with retailers. [WEAK SIGNAL · Algérie360, undated; P3 §2]
- Foreign suppliers demand secured or advance payment from Algerian importers. [WEAK SIGNAL · Le360 2021; F-COST §5]
- Algerian e-shops (assly, mabricole) sell B2C cash on delivery; Leader Aluminium's store states payment on delivery. [VERIFIED n.d. · snippets; F-COST §5; P2-JC]
- No survey gives typical 30/60/90-day terms, DSO or the share of B2B sales on credit in Algeria; the World Bank Enterprise Survey for Algeria dates from 2007. [UNKNOWN · P3 §2]

## 3. Why they switch, why they stay

- Import controls (Algex prior approval since 2022, PPI, prior domiciliation with 120% provision from May 2026) caused input and spare-part shortages. [STRONG SIGNAL · see seed 01; P3 §3]
- Car spare parts became "increasingly rare", a "parcours du combattant". [STRONG SIGNAL · TSA; P3 §3]
- Buyers' documented responses: vertical integration (Venus, reportedly), local supplier development (Henkel "wall-to-wall" suppliers inside the Réghaïa complex; ~70% integration), and the cabas channel. [STRONG SIGNAL (Henkel) · WEAK SIGNAL (Venus) · Afrik.com, Algérie Eco; P3 §3]
- Industrial subcontracting is described as "embryonic"; officials call for "a true relationship of trust" between large contractors and local subcontractors. [STRONG SIGNAL · Algérie360; P3 §3, §5]
- Public-company managers are "frileux" about national subcontractors, letting foreign firms win. [STRONG SIGNAL · Algérie Eco 2017; P3 §5]
- ENIEM's AC start slipped from Aug to Dec 2024 because of administrative and Red Sea logistics delays. [VERIFIED 2024-11-21 · Algérie Eco; P3 §3]
- No Algerian evidence exists on supplier relationship length, single vs multiple sourcing, or stockpiling after 2021. [UNKNOWN · P3 §3]

## 4. Who funds molds

- No Algerian source on mold-funding norms (customer vs molder, ownership) was found. [UNKNOWN · P3 §4]
- International (China) norm: production tooling US$5,000–15,000+ (multi-cavity), prototypes US$1,000–3,000; common payment split 50/40/10; the contract should state the mold is the buyer's property or the factory may refuse to release it. [VERIFIED 2026-10-03 · Haizol, NewBuyingAgent, ChinaImportal; F-MOLD §3; P3 §4]
- SIPEM advertises customer-specific projects "from technical study to mold realisation". [STRONG SIGNAL · Ouedkniss snippet; P2-CL §1]

## 5. What builds trust

- Documented trust levers are institutional only: a complete administrative file, local-integration programmes (Henkel), and the official call for trust in subcontracting. [VERIFIED / STRONG SIGNAL · P3 §5]
- Fairs act as meeting points: Batimatec, SIPSA-Filaha, ALGEST, SANIST. [VERIFIED · P3 §5]
- Perception of "Made in Algeria" quality for industrial parts is not documented (only food-product consumer studies). [UNKNOWN · P3 §5]
- Personal introductions ("maarifa"), regional and family networks, sampling and trial-order sizes are not documented online. [UNKNOWN · P3 §5]

## Assumptions (not facts)

- Two buying regimes: owner-led SMEs (one meeting can go from introduction to price and terms; risk is personal payment discipline and whim) and groups/multinationals (purchasing file → QA sample → trial order → approved list, 3–9 months; real veto sits with QA/R&D). [ASSUMPTION · P3 §1]
- Buying-centre roles by sector (initiator, technical influencer, buyer, payer, veto) are inferred, e.g. SME detergent filler: owner buys and signs cheques, production manager tests on the filling line, incumbent bottle/cap trader is the gatekeeper; joinery: distributor owner controls the shelf; maintenance: urgency overrides the approved-supplier list. [ASSUMPTION · P3 §1 table]
- A large buyer's "no" may mean "we will make it ourselves". [ASSUMPTION · P3 §1]
- Default terms a new molder could offer: new SMEs pay cash, certified cheque or transfer before/on delivery for the first 2–3 orders, then 30 days by traite avalisée or post-dated cheque; groups 60 days by transfer (reliable but slow); public buyers in distress: advance payment or nothing; never more than one month of a customer's purchases outstanding. [ASSUMPTION · P3 §2, §6]
- Model distributor credit at 30–60 days, often by post-dated cheque, with a bad-debt provision. [ASSUMPTION · F-COST §5]
- The Dec 2025–Jan 2026 cash squeeze probably lengthened distributors' payment cycles and raised requests for credit days in 2026. [ASSUMPTION · P3 §2]
- Do not plan on factoring in 2026–27. [ASSUMPTION · P3 §2]
- The strongest switch trigger in 2022–2026 is availability failure (stock-outs, blocked imports), not price; retention comes from holding stock and never stocking out. [ASSUMPTION mixed with STRONG SIGNAL · P3 §3]
- Buyers burned by stock-outs will dual-source, keeping the import or cabas source as backup. [ASSUMPTION · P3 §3]
- Cabas wins on one-off, brand-name, high-value-per-kg parts; a local molder wins on volume, bulky parts, custom fit and an invoice. [ASSUMPTION · P3 §3]
- Sector switch triggers (hypotheses): personal care — stock-out, MOQ 100k+, colour, blocked import, leaking caps; joinery — distributor stock-out, DZD depreciation, customs delay; irrigation — pre-season stock-out; furniture — colour and small quantities; appliances — integration targets and CKD delays; maintenance — machine down with no original part. [ASSUMPTION · P3 §3 table]
- Tooling options to offer: (1) customer-funded, customer-owned mold, 50% at order / 50% at sample approval, with ownership and storage clause — groups accept, owner-led SMEs often do not; (2) shared/amortised mold, 30–50% upfront and the rest over the first N pieces with a minimum-volume commitment (unenforceable if the buyer disappears); (3) molder-funded stock mold for universal parts. Grant exclusivity only on customer-funded molds, time-limited to 12–24 months. [ASSUMPTION · P3 §4]
- Small owner-led buyers resist paying for molds upfront, a candidate explanation of the shampoo-cap gap. [ASSUMPTION · P3 §4]
- Trust builders to test: show the machine and part; introduction by someone the buyer knows; complete admin file at first meeting; free samples on the real mold then a paid trial; local stock with delivery in days; DZD price stability; first known customer as reference; ISO 9001 only for multinationals. [ASSUMPTION · P3 §5]
- Value proposition ranking by evidence weight: availability > fit/custom > MOQ flexibility > price. [ASSUMPTION · P3 §6]
