# Seed 07 — Global analogies: how comparable molders started, and how Algeria compares with peer countries (as of 2026-10-04)

**Context.** Company histories of molders and component makers that began small, and a country comparison (Algeria vs Morocco, Tunisia, Egypt, Turkey, China, UAE). Use as analogies for a one-press (100–120 t) B2B entrant in Algeria. Seed document for a multi-agent simulation.

## Assumptions (not facts)

- Recurring pattern: successful component makers began with one machine (or a handful), one narrow repeat product and one anchor customer type, often from an adjacent non-molding business (cork, PVC blinds, trading, combs); distribution and customer relationships came before molding capacity. [ASSUMPTION · P5 §1]
- Catalogue players (Caplugs, KAPSTO, Elesa, Essentra) are mature, built over 50–80 years; their same-day stock model only pays once a mold library is amortised. [ASSUMPTION · P5 §1]
- Moats came from range + availability + standardisation + service, or from patents in strong-IP countries — not from molding itself. [ASSUMPTION · P5 §1]
- Copy to Algeria: start narrow with one anchor buyer; MUD/insert-frame tooling so each new part is an insert (also shrinks the 120% bank provision per import); family molds for kits; free samples plus a printed/PDF catalogue with drawings; a written two-tier tooling policy (molder-owned catalogue tools vs customer-funded tools that stay at the molder, with buy-out and release terms); installer education (Tigre); trading-first bridging while molds are on order (Jain, Pöppelmann). [ASSUMPTION · P5 §2]
- Will fail in Algeria (now): instant online quoting and marketplaces (CAD literacy unconfirmed, card payment rare, one press cannot absorb the software); same-day mega-catalogues (each tool needs domiciliation, 120% provision, PPI); patent moats (IP rank 53/55); "rapid" tooling (25–45 working days in China becomes 3–4 months landed); vertical PET preform + closure integration; early export-led growth. [ASSUMPTION · P5 §2]
- Adapt: a 10–30 SKU local-stock catalogue on 2–4 insert frames or family molds; copy defence from physical control of molds, ownership contracts, replenishment speed, colour/QC consistency and engraved brand/date codes; 48–72 h quotes from a sample, photo or drawing; tooling deposits before cutting steel (Algerian acceptance unknown); import 1–2 precision frames once, then cut inserts locally (El Eulma/Sétif, Boumerdès); Shaily-style anchor qualification with an OEM under integration pressure for small parts, building first-article and dimensional reports before IATF. [ASSUMPTION · P5 §2]
- Algeria wins on running cost (energy, labour) and loses on capital-cycle friction (FX premium, 120% provision, PPI, ≤5-year machine rule): favour few long-running low-changeover parts, minimise import events, hold more resin safety stock than a Moroccan or Tunisian peer. [ASSUMPTION · P5 §3]
- Morocco and Tunisia grew via FDI/export into European auto and electronics; Algeria's route is policy-driven domestic import substitution. Egypt and Turkey grew as family groups adding processes and exporting regionally; their suppliers sell into Algeria and are potential import competitors if protection weakens. China is the tool and machine source, not a peer; the UAE is mainly a resin/masterbatch trading hub. [ASSUMPTION · P5 §3]
- Scaling rule: add presses from cash flow when a family runs two shifts; add tonnage only when an anchor customer commits volume (Shaily–IKEA; Şanal waited five years for machine 2); consider export (Tunisia, Libya, Sahel) only after QC documentation matures. [ASSUMPTION · P5 §4]
