# Scenarios to simulate — Algeria one-press plastic-component entry (Phase 6)

Fifteen scenarios. Each has a precise setup, the variables to vary, the observable outcomes to report, and the evidence each outcome must be compared against. Use them for (a) the analyst's transparent role-play with `personas.md` and (b) MiroFish runs (the prediction-requirement texts are in `README.md`).

**Output rule (applies to every scenario).** Every result is labelled **SIMULATED OUTCOME**. It is never evidence. Each simulated outcome must be put next to the evidence in seeds 01–07 and marked *consistent*, *contradicted* or *no evidence*. An outcome that rests on `[ASSUMPTION]` persona behaviour must say so.

## 1. Quiet one-machine entry

- **Setup.** Founder installs the press and sells only through direct visits and introductions, no advertising, no Ouedkniss listing. Starts with 2 families. Personas driving: hardware wholesaler, joinery accessory distributor, irrigation tube maker, incumbent molder, copycat, machine distributor.
- **Vary.** (a) First family: spacers / packers / drip fittings / flip-top caps. (b) Channel: direct to producers vs through 1–2 wholesalers. (c) Price: equal to import landed / −10% / −20%. (d) Introduction route: none vs via resin distributor or mold maker ("maarifa").
- **Observe.** Months to reach 48, 107 and 150 machine-h/month; utilisation at months 6, 12, 18; first reorder date; month in which any competitor or dealer becomes aware of the niche; peak cash need; which persona type converts first and why.
- **Compare against.** No named-buyer pain found in any sector [UNKNOWN · S04]; availability is the strongest switch trigger [ASSUMPTION/STRONG · S05 §3]; East Algiers is the densest copier cluster [ASSUMPTION · S02].

## 2. Shampoo / dispensing-cap niche

- **Setup.** Founder buys one 8–12 cavity flip-top mold for 28/410 (and later 24/410), offers colour from 5k pcs, and sells to SME cosmetic fillers directly and through EBM bottle blowers. Personas driving: shampoo factory owner, detergent manufacturer, bottle blower, import trader, incumbent molder (SIPEM-like), end consumer, Chinese mold maker.
- **Vary.** (a) MOQ per colour: 5k / 10k / 20k. (b) Channel: direct to brands vs bundled with a blower. (c) Price per 1,000 relative to trader price: −20% / equal / +10% with faster delivery. (d) Incumbent response: SIPEM-like molder already offers flip-tops at low MOQ vs does not. (e) Hinge quality: no defects vs 2% hinge failures in month 3. (f) Neck reality: brands use standard 24/410–28/410 vs non-standard necks.
- **Observe.** Number of brands adopting; monthly cap volume; price achieved vs FOB × 237 (official) and × 428 (parallel) landed; share sold via blowers; complaints and lost brands after a quality incident; whether any brand offers to fund a custom mold.
- **Compare against.** Founder anecdote [WEAK SIGNAL · S04 §1]; no Algerian flip-top maker indexed [WEAK SIGNAL]; SIPEM claims dispenser caps [STRONG SIGNAL · S02]; closures line 3923.50.92 DD 30%, no DAPS seen [STRONG SIGNAL · S01 §3]; local cost ≈2.2–2.75 DZD vs ≈4.7–5.9 DZD landed at official FX [ASSUMPTION · S04].

## 3. Competitor copies after 6 months

- **Setup.** Baseline entry (scenario 1, best variant). At month 6 a copycat entrant buys the same press and a mold for the founder's best-selling SKU, from a sample bought at a wholesaler. Personas driving: copycat, local mold maker, hardware wholesaler, joinery distributor, procurement manager, founder's customers.
- **Vary.** (a) What is copied: a catalogue SKU vs a part made to one customer's drawing. (b) Copycat price cut: 10% / 20% / 30%. (c) Founder defence: none / match price / hold price + stock and 48 h delivery / supply contracts with traites / mold kept in-house with engraved date codes. (d) Copy speed: local mold in 6 weeks vs Chinese mold in 3–4 months.
- **Observe.** Founder's share of the copied SKU at months 9, 12, 18; price erosion; margin; which customers defect (by persona) and stated reason; whether the copycat spreads to a second SKU.
- **Compare against.** Copying capability exists (mold makers make parts "from models", CDTA reverse engineering) [STRONG SIGNAL · S02 §6]; no documented copying case [UNKNOWN]; Algeria IP Index 25.96, rank 53/55 [VERIFIED 2026 · S07]; copy risk highest for catalogue items [ASSUMPTION · S02].

## 4. 3 → 10 → 20 molds on one press

- **Setup.** Founder adds molds over 18 months: 3 at start, 10 by month 9, 20 by month 18. Personas driving: buyers across sectors, local and Chinese mold makers, bank officer (each import), customs broker.
- **Vary.** (a) Tooling type: full molds vs insert frames (DME MUD-type, vendor-claimed changeover <5 min). (b) Mold source: China (3–4 months landed, 120% provision) vs local (≈6 weeks). (c) Demand per SKU: high-runner vs long-tail. (d) Stocking: make-to-order vs 2–4 weeks finished-goods stock.
- **Observe.** Changeovers per month and hours lost; utilisation; finished-goods inventory (DZD); cash tied in molds and provisions; stock-outs per SKU; month in which a second press is justified (press 1 >75% use, per the project rule).
- **Compare against.** 120% provision ≥30 days before each shipment [VERIFIED 2026-05-14 · S01 §4]; China T1 25–45 working days [VERIFIED · S06 §3]; insert-tooling claims are vendor claims [VERIFIED (claim only) · S07]; utilisation dominates unit cost [ASSUMPTION · S06].

## 5. Finished-part import restrictions tighten

- **Setup.** From month 3, the state tightens imports of finished plastic parts. Personas driving: import trader (incl. cabas), hardware wholesaler, joinery distributor, irrigation tube maker, procurement manager, customs broker, copycat.
- **Vary.** (a) Mechanism: DAPS 60% extended to 3925/3926.90/3917 / PPI refusals for finished plastic parts / Instruction 05-2026 tightened / cabas crackdown. (b) Severity: mild (one mechanism) vs severe (all). (c) Founder capacity: one press only vs subcontract overflow to an SNK-like molder.
- **Observe.** Inbound enquiries; price umbrella (import landed price) change; founder price increases accepted; capacity shortfall and lost orders; new local entrants; traders converting to distributors of local parts.
- **Compare against.** DAPS legal base and 60% on 3923.10/3926.40.90 [VERIFIED/STRONG · S01 §3]; DAPS status on target lines unknown [UNKNOWN]; Algex 2022 shortages [STRONG SIGNAL · S05 §3]; 2021 resin crisis precedent of policy-driven stoppage [VERIFIED c.2021 · S03 §4]; GPI precedent of closure by fiat [STRONG SIGNAL · S01 §1].

## 6. Resin price +20% / +50% / +100%

- **Setup.** At month 4 resin rises from the baseline price. Personas driving: resin distributor, all buyers, incumbent molder, import trader.
- **Vary.** (a) Increase: +20% / +50% / +100%. (b) Supply: available at a price vs rationed (2-week gaps). (c) Founder buffer: 0 / 4 / 8 weeks of stock. (d) Contract: fixed DZD price for 3 months vs resin-indexed clause. (e) Regrind share in non-cosmetic parts: 0% / 20%.
- **Observe.** Margin per family; price increases accepted vs customers lost; stoppage days; cash impact of carrying stock; which families become unprofitable (compare with the model's "KILL" rule: negative margin at 400 DZD/kg and 50% utilisation).
- **Compare against.** 2021: 170 → 520 DZD/kg (>300%), plants stopped for months [VERIFIED c.2021 · S03 §3–4]; 2026: PP +38%, PE +37% world [STRONG SIGNAL · S03 §3]; no local DZD/kg quote [UNKNOWN]; resin ≈50–70% of ex-works cost [ASSUMPTION · S03].

## 7. Chinese imports become easier

- **Setup.** From month 6 import friction eases. Personas driving: import trader, wholesaler, procurement manager, Chinese mold maker, bank officer.
- **Vary.** (a) Mechanism: Note 01/DGC/2026 relaxed (no 120% provision) / PPI abolished / DD on finished plastics cut from 30% to 15% / official FX access widened for traders. (b) Combination: one vs all.
- **Observe.** Import landed price vs founder price per family; buyers returning to imports (who, when, why); founder price cuts needed to hold volume; utilisation; whether service (stock, 48 h delivery) retains customers.
- **Compare against.** No relaxation of the note found to Oct 2026 [STRONG SIGNAL · S01 §4]; FX gap ≈80% [VERIFIED 2026 · S06 §1]; retail of construction consumables at 3–7× landed floor [ASSUMPTION · S04]; Egyptian/Turkish suppliers as latent import competitors [ASSUMPTION · S07].

## 8. Customers demand 60/90-day credit

- **Setup.** From month 3 distributors and groups demand longer terms. Personas driving: hardware wholesaler, joinery distributor, procurement manager, furniture manufacturer (public variant), bank officer.
- **Vary.** (a) Terms demanded: 60 vs 90 days. (b) Share of revenue affected: 30% / 60% / 90%. (c) Instrument: post-dated cheque vs traite avalisée vs transfer. (d) Bounce rate on cheques: 0% / 5% / 15%. (e) Founder policy: accept / refuse / accept only with traite and one-month exposure cap.
- **Observe.** DSO; peak working capital; bad debts; sales lost by refusing; overdraft cost at ≈7.5%; months of runway.
- **Compare against.** No survey of credit days exists [UNKNOWN · S05 §2]; bounced-cheque regime and 2016 incident volume [STRONG SIGNAL · S05]; public payer delays [STRONG SIGNAL]; factoring regulated only Aug 2026 [STRONG SIGNAL]; cash-to-cheque chain [STRONG SIGNAL].

## 9. Machine fails for 2 / 7 / 30 days

- **Setup.** The only press stops in month 8 (peak delivery period for at least one family). Personas driving: machine distributor, incumbent molder (as backup), affected buyers, maintenance manager (as a buyer that values speed).
- **Vary.** (a) Duration: 2 / 7 / 30 days. (b) Cause: heater/thermocouple (spare kit on hand) vs controller board (1–3 weeks courier from China) vs hydraulic pump. (c) Dealer support: bought via local dealer vs direct import with no local technician. (d) Backup: finished-goods stock of 0 / 2 / 4 weeks; molds runnable at an SNK-like subcontractor (interface matched) or not.
- **Observe.** Late deliveries; customers who activate their old/import source and whether they come back; cost of subcontracting the gap; recovery time; reputational effect on reorders.
- **Compare against.** AFC/Yizumi local commissioning evidence [VERIFIED · S02 §4]; no independent repair business found [UNKNOWN]; controller boards sourced from China in 1–3 weeks [ASSUMPTION · S06]; SNK-like shop runs customer molds [VERIFIED · S02].

## 10. Start in Algiers vs Sétif/El Eulma vs Bordj Bou Arréridj vs Oran

- **Setup.** Same founder, same product menu, four alternative locations. Personas driving: all buyers by sector, local mold maker, machine distributor, resin distributor, incumbent molder, copycat.
- **Vary.** Location: East Algiers–Boumerdès / Sétif–El Eulma / BBA / Oran. Per location, toggle the nearest buyers (personal care in Blida–Algiers; joinery in Hammedi and BBA; irrigation in Aïn Arnat and Boufarik; appliances in BBA–Sétif), mold makers, dealers, resin distributor (Zéralda / Skikda / Oran), and competitor density.
- **Observe.** Time to first 5 customers; delivery distance to the main buyers; days to repair a mold or machine; copy events; resin lead time; rent and logistics cost (flag as unknown).
- **Compare against.** Cluster map [STRONG SIGNAL · S02 §1]; Oran shows only commodity molders [STRONG SIGNAL]; resin distributors in Skikda, Zéralda, Es Senia [VERIFIED · S03 §2]; rent data absent [UNKNOWN · S06 §1]; El Eulma "wholesale hub" claim unsourced [ASSUMPTION].

## 11. Customer funds the mold

- **Setup.** For every new part, the founder asks the customer to pay for the mold (customer owns it on paper; it stays at the founder's shop with maintenance included). Personas driving: shampoo owner, detergent manufacturer, procurement manager, maintenance manager, joinery distributor, Chinese and local mold makers, bank officer.
- **Vary.** (a) Funding split: 100% upfront / 50% at order + 50% at sample approval / 30% upfront + rest amortised over first N pieces. (b) Buyer type: owner-led SME vs group. (c) Mold release terms: free removal vs buy-out fee + notice. (d) Mold source and payment route under domiciliation.
- **Observe.** Acceptance rate by buyer type; number of new parts launched; founder capex avoided; molds removed to other molders; disputes; cash timing vs the 120% provision.
- **Compare against.** Algerian tooling-funding norms unknown [UNKNOWN · S05 §4]; China norm customer-owned, 50/40/10 [VERIFIED · S05/S06]; Protolabs two-tier ownership model [VERIFIED · S07]; SME resistance to upfront mold payment [ASSUMPTION · S05].

## 12. We fund the mold

- **Setup.** Founder funds all molds (molder-owned) and recovers cost through part price. Personas driving: same as 11 plus copycat and bank officer.
- **Vary.** (a) Molds funded: 3 / 6 / 10 in year 1. (b) Payback target: 6 / 12 / 24 months of volume. (c) Price premium to cover amortisation: 0% / 10% / 20%. (d) Financing: equity vs overdraft (≈7.5%) vs leasing (≈10.1%).
- **Observe.** Capex and peak cash; parts sold per mold vs planned; molds that never pay back; copy exposure on molder-owned designs; utilisation.
- **Compare against.** Mold prices USD 5–15k+ (China) [VERIFIED · S06 §3]; credit rates [VERIFIED · S06 §1]; mold amortisation dominates micro-part cost at low volume [ASSUMPTION · S04/S06].

## 13. Exclusive customer mold

- **Setup.** One anchor customer funds a mold for a part made to its drawing and gets exclusivity. Personas driving: procurement manager (or detergent manufacturer), copycat, incumbent molder, bank officer.
- **Vary.** (a) Exclusivity: 12 vs 24 months vs unlimited. (b) Volume commitment: none / minimum annual quantity backed by traites. (c) Anchor's share of founder revenue: 30% / 50% / 70%. (d) Anchor pays at 60 vs 90 days.
- **Observe.** Revenue concentration; founder cash if the anchor delays payment or cuts volume; behaviour when exclusivity ends (stay, re-tender, in-source); copy attempts by other molders.
- **Compare against.** Shaily–IKEA ≈50% anchor pattern [VERIFIED · S07]; make-vs-buy at large buyers [VERIFIED/WEAK · S05 §1]; public payment delays and ENIEM [STRONG/VERIFIED · S05]; exclusivity time-limit proposal 12–24 months [ASSUMPTION · S05].

## 14. Standard catalogue product

- **Setup.** Founder launches a "catalogue-lite" of 10–30 stocked SKUs (spacers in several cover sizes, packers, drainage caps, drip fittings) with free samples and a printed/PDF catalogue, sold through wholesalers. Personas driving: hardware wholesaler, joinery distributor, irrigation tube maker, import trader, copycat.
- **Vary.** (a) SKU count: 10 / 20 / 30. (b) Stock cover: 2 / 4 / 8 weeks. (c) Channel: 2 wholesalers vs 6 wholesalers vs direct. (d) Delivery promise: 48 h vs 1 week.
- **Observe.** Sell-through per SKU; inventory days and cash in stock; stock-outs; wholesaler loyalty; copy events per SKU; contribution per machine-hour.
- **Compare against.** Caplugs/Essentra/KAPSTO catalogue + samples model [VERIFIED · S07]; catalogue players matured over 50–80 years [ASSUMPTION · S07]; retail spacer and tile-clip prices [VERIFIED · S04 §3]; catalogue items most exposed to copying [ASSUMPTION · S02].

## 15. Competitor price war

- **Setup.** At month 8, an incumbent molder (SNK-like) or a copycat cuts prices 20–40% on the founder's two main families. Personas driving: incumbent molder, copycat, wholesaler, joinery distributor, irrigation tube maker, procurement manager, bank officer.
- **Vary.** (a) Attacker: incumbent with amortised machines vs new copycat with debt. (b) Cut: 20% / 30% / 40%. (c) Duration: 3 / 6 / 12 months. (d) Founder response: match / partial match + service / hold price + contracts / exit the family and redeploy press hours.
- **Observe.** Months of survival at each response; cash trough; share retained; customers who stay for service and why; attacker's sustainability (does it raise prices again?).
- **Compare against.** Incumbent capacity (50–650 t, mold design) [VERIFIED · S02]; competitor pricing behaviour unknown [UNKNOWN · S02]; household plastics already a price war vs imports and counterfeits [WEAK SIGNAL · S02 §5]; value ranking availability > fit > MOQ > price [ASSUMPTION · S05].
