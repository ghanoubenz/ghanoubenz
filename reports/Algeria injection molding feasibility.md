# Validate Products Before Buying Machines in Algeria

**Executive answer.** The lowest-risk, lowest-capital and fastest route is not "buy a Chinese machine, then find products". It is **"own the molds, rent the machine-hours, own the customer"**. For 6–9 months, sell a small family of import-substituting B2B consumables made under subcontract. Buy a press only after you hold purchase orders worth about **110–150 machine-hours per month**, which is roughly one shift of steady work. The best first family is concrete and formwork accessories plus aluminium-joinery glazing packers, sold through building-material wholesalers. A second family, 16 mm drip-irrigation fittings, follows. All of these run in PP/HDPE on a 120 t servo press.

The capital need is real. About **USD 10–25k** gets you to validated, invoiced demand through subcontracting. About **USD 45–55k** in total is needed by month 12 once you add a 120 t servo press and the day-one auxiliaries, with 70% of the machine financed. Year-1 peak funding in the model below is **≈6.2M DZD (≈USD 46k at the official rate)**.

The model you started with is directionally right on three points. Algeria imports all of its PP and HDPE. Duties protect local parts heavily: about 5% on machines against **30% plus a reported 60% DAPS** on finished plastic articles. Chinese 120 t servo presses cost about **USD 14k FOB**.

It is wrong on sequencing and on moat:

- **Machine before customer.** About 1,000 Algerian converters already exist, roughly 40% of them in injection, and AAPI has registered **675 new plastics projects** since 2022. Capacity is not scarce; qualified demand is.
- **Banking is a critical-path item.** Bank of Algeria Note 01/DGC/2026 (14 May 2026) requires **domiciliation before shipment, at least 30 days ahead, with a 120% provision**. Every Chinese mold or machine therefore carries a fixed 30+ day banking lead.
- **Local molds are underrated.** Paid in DZD, they skip the domiciliation, FX and customs steps entirely. For simple first molds that can outweigh a price premium.

Almost every Algerian website was blocked to the researchers, so most facts here come from search-engine snippets. Prices, MOQs, contacts and duty rates must be confirmed by phone or visit before you commit money; §16 gives the checklist.

### 2.5 TDS explainer: why "PP" is not a specification

### 3.1 Scoring model (transparent, reproducible)

Each of the 112 long-list items, all from the research long list and ESTIMATE-grade, was scored from 1 to 5 on your 13 criteria. The scores are derived mechanically from the long-list fields:
- press tonnage and mold complexity

- technical and regulatory difficulty
- part weight
- resin family
- an estimated price-per-gram ratio against resin cost, as a margin proxy, with a −2 penalty where Chinese imports flood the market (cable ties, wall plugs, tile spacers, coat hooks)

Competition scores 4 when no Algerian maker was found (absence of evidence ≠ evidence of absence), 3 where local players are likely, and 1–2 where the competitor is confirmed (SGT caps, PPR fittings).

### 5.1 Process chain from idea to production

7. Mold design approval: 2D/3D layout, steel, cooling.
8. **Bank domiciliation, if imported: ≥30 days before shipment.**
9. Steel cutting.
10. T0 internal shot.
11. T1 samples plus dimensional report.
12. Corrections, then T2/T3.
13. Final ("golden") sample approval.
14. Pre-shipment inspection.
15. Rust-proof packing and shipment.
16. Customs.
17. Trial on the Algerian press.
18. Customer approval (PPAP-lite: dimensions, function, colour).
19. Production.

### 5.2 Glossary (brief)

- **Cavity / core**: the female and male halves that form the part.
- **Cavitation**: parts per shot.
- **Runner / sprue**: channels that feed the plastic. A **cold runner** is reground as scrap; a **hot runner** has no runner scrap and costs USD 3–15k more (VERIFIED, [Boxu](https://www.boxumold.com/blog/plastic-mold-pricing-guide)).
- **Gate**: the entry point into the cavity.
- **Slide / lifter / core-pull**: moving parts that form undercuts.
- **Ejector pins / stripper plate**: push the part out.
- **Mold base**: standard plates, e.g. LKM or HASCO.
- **Insert**: replaceable steel block. Used to make families, such as spacer sizes or packer thicknesses, from one base.
- **Locating ring / sprue bushing radius**: the interface with the press nozzle.
- **SPI class**: life rating. 101 is >1M cycles, 102 is 500k–1M, 103 is <500k, 104 is <100k (VERIFIED, [Texas Injection Molding](https://texasinjectionmolding.com/society-of-the-plastic-industry-mold-building-standards/)).
- **Steels**: P20/718H are pre-hardened, about 28–34 HRC. H13/S136 are hardened to 48–52 HRC. 718H costs +15–25% over P20 and S136 +40–60% (VERIFIED, [Boxu](https://www.boxumold.com/blog/plastic-mold-pricing-guide)).
- **T0/T1/T2**: successive trial rounds. Industry usage; no source found.

### 5.3 Mold–machine interface (must match **both** the subcontractor's press and your future press)

### 5.7 The banking critical path (Note 01/DGC/2026)

- All goods imports must be **domiciled before shipment**.
- Transport documents must post-date the domiciliation.
- A **120% provision** must be lodged **≥30 days before shipment**.
- Domiciliation after shipment is accepted only by exception.

(VERIFIED, [Banque d'Algérie PDF](https://www.bank-of-algeria.dz/stoodroa/2026/05/note-01-DGC-2026.pdf); [Algérie-Eco 14 May 2026](https://algerie-eco.com/2026/05/14/importations-la-banque-dalgerie-impose-la-domiciliation-bancaire-avant-lexpedition-des-marchandises/).)

Service imports (stand-alone design or Moldflow) also need domiciliation and may trigger withholding tax (NOT verified). **Buy design and DFM bundled inside the mold price.**

**Consequences:**
- (a) Open a business account and talk to the trade-finance desk in **week 1**.
- (b) Each imported mold or machine needs **120% of its value in DZD parked** about a month before it ships. That is a working-capital hit not shown on supplier quotes.
- (c) Ask the bank whether the 30% deposit can go by transfer or must use a documentary instrument.
- (d) Paying through an agent's personal account or in parallel-market euros is outside the legal framework and leaves no customs-valuation proof. **Do not.**

Formal import at **133 DZD/USD** is ≈45% cheaper than buying the same dollars at ≈240.

## 8. Costing model: how a molder actually makes money

### 8.1 The mechanism

A molder sells **machine-hours converted into parts**. Revenue per hour = parts per hour × price. Cost per hour is mostly fixed: people, depreciation, rent. Material is mostly pass-through. Profit therefore comes from five levers:
1. **Utilisation**: hours sold out of hours available.
2. **Parts per hour**: cavities ÷ cycle time × OEE.
3. **Material yield**: runner regrind, low scrap, correct part weight.
4. **Resin purchasing**: grade substitution, regrind on non-critical parts, bulk lots.
5. **Pricing against the customer's alternative**: imported landed cost, which includes 30% duty, DAPS and the parallel FX premium.

A tool that runs 8 cavities at 18 s earns 4–8× more per hour than a 1-cavity tool at 35 s on the same press. **Cavitation and utilisation decide the business, not the machine price.**

### 8.2 Formula (reusable)

> **Unit cost = Material + Conversion + Mold + Packaging + Transport (+ selling overhead in price)**
>
> - Material = part g × (1 + loss%) × blended resin DZD/kg ÷ 1000 ÷ yield
> - Blended resin = (1 − MB%) × resin + MB% × masterbatch price
> - Conversion = machine-hour rate ÷ (cavities × 3600 ÷ cycle s × yield)
> - Machine-hour rate = (depreciation + labour + rent + admin + financing + maintenance + electricity) ÷ productive hours
> - Mold = landed mold cost ÷ parts planned over the amortisation period
> - Price = Unit cost ÷ (1 − selling overhead%) ÷ (1 − target gross margin%)

## 10. Moat design when anyone can buy the same USD 14k press

1. **Mold portfolio plus speed of new tools.** Each mold is a 6–12 week barrier. Families built on interchangeable inserts let you add sizes cheaply (a new spacer cover size is an insert, not a mold). Keep 2–3 local mold makers on retainer for fast changes.
2. **Become the stock-holder.** Import controls (PPI, domiciliation, DAPS) make importers' supply lumpy. Promise **48-hour delivery from stock** to wholesalers, with mixed pallets across SKUs. A Chinese container cannot match that.
3. **"Show us the part you import" engineering service.** Reverse-engineering, prototyping (with CDTA) and low-volume tooling for factories' imported spare parts. This creates **customer-owned molds that live in your shop**, which is the strongest lock-in in molding.
4. **Customer-specific variants.** Colour-matched screw caps for each board brand, branded spacers for wholesalers, and private-label packaging make switching costly.
5. **Cost position from regrind and purchasing.** Use recycled PP/HDPE in spacers and keep 2–3 resin suppliers.
6. **Formal-sector credentials.** RC, NIF, VAT invoices and CNAS certificates (§13) are required by large buyers. Many informal workshops cannot invoice, so formality is a moat with OEMs and public-works contractors.
7. **Data:** know every customer's monthly consumption and refill before they ask.

## Conclusion

The decisive insight is that in Algeria in 2026 the scarce resources are **qualified demand, compliant import paperwork and cash timing**. Machine capacity and technology are not scarce. Three policies push the same way:
- the domiciliation rule (120%, 30 days ahead)
- the PPI
- DAPS and the 30% duty on finished articles

Together they make **importing parts painful for your customers and importing tools slow for you**. The winning position therefore sits between the two: a formal, invoicing, stock-holding local supplier with a growing library of molds, many of them made locally and paid in dinars. The press becomes a later, financeable, low-risk purchase once its hours are pre-sold.

Your original model treated the Chinese machine as the starting asset. The evidence says the starting assets are a **validated customer list, a family of inserts-based molds and a banker who understands your import calendar**. Two further cautions:
- Several "obvious" import-substitution items are already priced too low for a newcomer: junction boxes at 40 DZD, cable ties at fractions of a dinar from China.
- **Bulky, low-glamour B2B consumables sold in families** offer the most defensible margin for a small molder.
