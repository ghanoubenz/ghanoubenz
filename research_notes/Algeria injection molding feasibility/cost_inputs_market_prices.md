## 1. Cost inputs: electricity, wages, social charges, rent, packaging, transport, financing, exchange rates

### Takeaway
Algerian energy and finance costs are low by international standards: electricity is about 4.7–5.5 DZD/kWh for businesses (about €0.03), and investment credit runs about 5.8–6.9%, with leasing about 10%. Labour is cheap: the legal minimum wage (SNMG) is 24,000 DZD gross/month from Jan 2026, plus about 25–26% employer CNAS. The dominant cost distortion is the exchange-rate gap. The official rate is about 155 DZD/EUR and 133 DZD/USD, against about 276 DZD/EUR and 240 DZD/USD on the parallel (square) market, a premium of about 78–80%. That gap inflates the real cost of imported resin, moulds and machines for anyone who cannot get bank FX.

### Inferences
- ESTIMATE: the parallel premium is about **+78% on EUR** (276 / 155) and **+80% on USD** (240 / 133). A formal importer with bank domiciliation pays the official rate. Small traders who import informally (cabas or under-invoicing) price at the parallel rate. The cost model should run resin and mould costs at both rates as a sensitivity.
- ESTIMATE: at 4.7–5.5 DZD/kWh, a 100 t hydraulic press drawing about 10–15 kW average costs about **50–80 DZD/hour in electricity** (€0.35–0.55/h). Energy is a minor cost line. These kW figures are typical engineering assumptions, not sourced.
- ESTIMATE: fully loaded cost of a minimum-wage operator = 24,000 × 1.26 ≈ **30,200 DZD/month**. Realistic private-sector operator pay of 32,000–45,000 net implies about 45,000–65,000 DZD loaded (range inferred from the JDN/Sakinadz figures above).
- ESTIMATE: for equipment, leasing at about 10% is materially dearer than medium/long-term bank credit at 5.8–6.9%, but it typically needs less collateral.

### Gaps

## 2. Subcontract injection pricing and machine-hour rates

### Takeaway
No published hourly machine rates for subcontract injection in Algeria were found. International benchmarks retrieved are thin and partly dated: a North American survey put 25–100 t presses at $9–35/hour (old data), and Chinese labour runs $3–8/hour against $30–60/hour in Europe.

### Cited Findings
- An IMM survey of 47 molders found hourly rates for **25–100 t presses from $9.07 to $35**. The survey is North American and old, with no date in the snippet. VERIFIED (snippet) — [PlasticsToday, "Pricing your work: the machine rate enigma"](https://www.plasticstoday.com/resin-pricing/pricing-your-work-the-machine-rate-enigma)
- Labour: China $3–8/h, USA $25–50/h, Europe $30–60/h. Chinese factory-gate prices are 30–60% below EU/NA. VERIFIED (snippet), vendor marketing source — [Rayleap 2025](https://rayleap.com/2025-injection-molding-costs-save-40-60-in-china-vs-usa-europe-real-case/)
- New 30–100 t machines cost about **$10,000–30,000** (Chinese price guides, 2025). VERIFIED (snippet), vendor source — [Daoben machinery price guide 2025](https://www.daobenmachinery.com/plastic-injection-molding-machine-price-guide-2025/)
- Context: an Ouedkniss ad offers "Fabrication de boîte d'encastrement" as a service in Boumerdès, which shows that local molders market custom injection. No price was visible. — [Ouedkniss ad](https://www.ouedkniss.com/fabrication-de-boîte-d-encastrement-boumerdes-algerie-services-d20630861?lang=fr)
- The Algerian subcontracting market is estimated at $1.64 bn, and the press describes the lack of a real subcontracting strategy. — [Algérie360](https://www.algerie360.com/sous-traitance-en-algerie-absence-dune-veritable-strategie/) (general context only)

### Inferences
- ESTIMATE: Algerian subcontract pricing is very likely quoted per piece, including material, at the parallel-rate-equivalent resin cost, rather than as a published hourly rate. A field survey (phone quotes from 3–5 molders in Sétif, Bordj Bou Arreridj and Oran) is needed.

### Gaps
- No DZD/hour or €/hour subcontract rates were found for Algeria, Turkey or China in 2025–2026. No current European machine-hour rates (e.g. VDMA or German Maschinenstundensatz surveys) were retrieved.

## 4. Chinese ex-works / FOB prices (import price floor)

### Takeaway
Chinese FOB prices are very low: about $1.5–3 per 1,000 standard 3.6×200 cable ties ($0.15–0.30 per bag of 100). Even after freight, 30% duty and 19% VAT at the official rate, the landed cost is about 50–60 DZD per 100 pcs, against 450 DZD retail. The DAPS safeguard duty (30–200%) and the parallel FX rate are the real determinants of the import floor.

### Cited Findings
- 3.6×200 nylon cable ties, 100 pcs/bag: **$0.15–0.23/bag FOB** (MOQ 100 bags). Others quote $0.28–0.30/bag (MOQ 250 bags), and a low of $0.06–0.08/bag. Factory-direct levelling clips are **$0.70–0.85/bag FOB Ningbo** (MOQ 500 bags). Spacers are **$0.20–0.35/bag**. ### Inferences
- ESTIMATE, cable-tie landed floor: $0.23/bag FOB × 133 DZD/USD = 30.6 DZD; + about 10% freight and insurance = 33.7; × 1.30 duty = 43.8; × 1.19 VAT = **about 52 DZD per bag of 100** at the official rate. At the parallel rate of 240, it is **about 94 DZD**. If DAPS (e.g. 60%) applies, the figure rises to roughly 75–135 DZD. All are far below the 450 DZD retail price, so distribution margins and brand positioning, not the import floor, set the retail price. The freight percentage and the DAPS example rate are assumptions.
- ESTIMATE: because the FOB price per piece of commodity cable ties is fractions of a DZD, a local molder competing on standard ties faces Chinese and Turkish price pressure. Bulky, low-value, high-freight items (rebar spacers, electrical boxes) and items with DAPS cover give better local margins.

### Gaps
- No FOB prices were found for junction or flush boxes, cable glands, conduit saddles, drip emitters, furniture glides, caps, pallet feet, hose connectors or pipe clips.
- Exact DD and DAPS rates for HS 3926.90, 3925, 3917.40 and 3923 were not retrieved, because upgrowth.dz and Douanes were blocked.

## 5. Working-capital norms in Algerian B2B trade

### Takeaway
No quantitative source was found for typical payment terms (cash versus 30/60/90 days) between Algerian manufacturers and quincailleries or distributors. Sources do confirm that cheques, bills of exchange and promissory notes are the main instruments, and that bounced cheques are a recognised and growing problem.

### Cited Findings
- Main payment methods in Algeria include company cheques, certified bank cheques, SWIFT transfers, bills of exchange, promissory notes, documentary collection and documentary credit. — [Crédit Agricole International, Algeria](https://international.groupecreditagricole.com/fr/accompagnement-a-l-international/notre-offre/73,algerie)
- Bounced cheques (chèques sans provision) are taking on "worrying proportions". Cheques are seen by users as an unsecured payment method, and few institutions use the Bank of Algeria's security device. — [Algérie360](https://www.algerie360.com/cheques-sans-provision-la-pratique-prend-des-proportions-inquietantes/)
- Foreign suppliers demand secured or advance payment from Algerian importers. — [Le360](https://afrique.le360.ma/algerie/economie/2021/03/13/33822-algerie-redoutant-des-defauts-de-paiement-les-fournisseurs-etrangers-exigent-des-paiements/) (2021)
- Algerian e-shops (assly.dz, mabricole) sell B2C with cash on delivery. — snippets in Section 3

### Inferences
- ESTIMATE, not sourced: a new small molder should model cash or cash on delivery for small quincailleries, 30–60 days (often by post-dated cheque) for established distributors, and a bad-debt or bounced-cheque provision. A field check is recommended.

### Gaps
- No survey data was found on DSO (days sales outstanding) or supplier-credit days in Algerian hardware or electrical distribution.
