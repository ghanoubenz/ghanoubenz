# Personal care, cosmetics and detergents: the closure (cap) case

Phase 1, Algeria reality map. Researched 2026-10-04.

**The question.** An Algerian shampoo maker reportedly could not find a local producer able or willing to supply a plastic cap. Is that a nationwide gap, or a specific problem of size, type, quality or MOQ?

## 1. Short answer (provisional)

**"Caps" in general are not a nationwide gap.** Commodity HDPE beverage closures are supplied at very large scale:
- SGT Algérie makes about 2 bn caps/yr at its Sétif cap plant, plus Rouiba. In 2015 it ran Husky HyCAP lines.
- PTD (Blida) is the No. 2 preform maker and has its own cap unit.
- Alpha PET and Tap Chihani also appear as cap makers.

**Supply of dispensing and cosmetic closures looks thin, poorly visible and partly imported.** This covers flip-top, disc-top, push-pull, pumps, triggers, and coloured or custom shapes for 24/410 and 28/410 necks.
- Only one local maker was found that explicitly claims dispenser caps *and* pumps: **SARL SIPEM** (ZI Oued Smar, Algiers, since 1982). The claim comes from an Ouedkniss ad snippet.
- Another claims capsules for pharma and cosmetics: **Laboratoires SPIC** (Mohammadia). This comes from a Kompass snippet.
- Traders such as **PAFIX** (Draria) and Ouedkniss sellers sell pumps and sprayers, and advertise importing cosmetic bottles.

**Demand for these closures has jumped because of policy.** The cosmetics import bill fell from more than USD 500M to **USD 58M in 2024**. Local industry covers about 70% of cosmetic needs. In January 2026 the minister declared "self-sufficiency" and named upstream inputs as the next challenge. About 250 Algerian firms exhibited at Cosmetica 2026.
- So hundreds of mostly small fillers suddenly need bottles and closures locally.
- Converters sized for beverage-scale volumes do not serve that kind of fragmented demand well.

**Best current hypothesis.** The shampoo maker's problem is most likely **specific, not general**. It is probably some combination of:
- (a) a dispensing closure type: a flip-top with hinge, or a disc-top;
- (b) a neck or shape that matches its particular bottle, which needs a custom mould nobody wants to fund;
- (c) MOQ: it needs perhaps 10–50k pieces per colour, while converters want 100k+ or a mould paid upfront;
- (d) colour matching or quality, such as hinge durability, leaks or capping torque.

These four are **not yet distinguished by evidence**. The founder must interview the shampoo maker; see section 7.

**Verdict: INVESTIGATE, leaning GO for a niche.** The niche would be stock-mould PP flip-top and disc-top closures in 24/410 and 28/410, with short runs and colour matching, sold with or through bottle blowers. Standard beverage caps are a **KILL** (SGT scale).

**Do buyers buy caps with bottles?** The Ouedkniss snippets show sets ("PET 50–100 ml bottles with caps or sprays"; "1 L bottle with pump, lot of 6,000"). That suggests small brands buy **bottle and closure as a set from one trader or blower** (WEAK SIGNAL). A closure startup should therefore sell **through or alongside EBM bottle makers**, which supply the neck, and not only direct to brands.

**Neck finishes.** No Algerian source specified the neck finishes. The industry standard for shampoo and gel is 24/410 and 28/410. PCO 28 (1881/1810) is a beverage finish and is not used on shampoo. Confirming the actual necks with the 2–3 largest local blowers is a priority. If the blowers each use non-standard necks, every closure needs a custom mould, which would explain the "nobody will do it" pain.

## 4. Evidence: general gap or specific gap?

**Against a general gap**
- About 2–3 bn beverage caps a year are made locally (SGT, PTD and others).
- Kompass lists "bouchons en plastique" makers in at least 10 wilayas.
- SIPEM (dispensing caps and pumps) and SPIC (cosmetic capsules) claim relevant capability.
- Venus may be self-supplying.

**For a specific gap (dispensing, cosmetic, custom, low MOQ)**
- Searches in French, Arabic and English for Algerian flip-top makers return **zero indexed producers**. Only Chinese and European suppliers came up (weak evidence, since SMEs are poorly indexed).
- Pumps and sprayers are visibly sold by traders and import services.
- The demand shock from import substitution (USD 500M to 58M) created hundreds of new filling SKUs in two years. Supply is unlikely to have kept pace in specialised closures.
- Fillers are fragmented (about 250 exhibitors; Labonedjma alone has 600 SKUs), so many orders are of small lots.
- A leader may have integrated packaging, which is a classic sign of unreliable outside supply.

**Current reading.** The gap is most likely **specific**. It lies in the overlap of *dispensing type* (flip-top or disc-top), *custom fit or colour* and *low-to-mid MOQ (10–100k)*. It is not "no one in Algeria makes caps". Confidence: **WEAK–STRONG SIGNAL**. It is not yet verified, because no buyer or supplier has been interviewed.

## 6. GO / INVESTIGATE / KILL implications

| Option | Call | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Standard beverage caps (PCO, 38 mm) | **KILL** | SGT and PTD operate at billion-unit scale with high-cavitation Husky systems |
| Pumps, triggers, fine-mist sprayers | **KILL for phase 1** | Multi-part, need springs and assembly; IP is held by large players; import-dominated |
| **Stock flip-top and disc-top caps 24/410 and 28/410 in PP; colour-matched; MOQ 10–20k per colour; sold with local bottle blowers** | **INVESTIGATE → likely GO** | Fits a 100–120 t press (4–8 cavity molds), and demand rose because of policy. Kill if 3 or more local suppliers already offer the same at low MOQ and at or below the landed import price. |
| Measuring caps for liquid laundry, jerrycan TE caps for detergent and bleach | INVESTIGATE (second line) | Large detergent volumes (Henkel, Hayat, UD, ENAD); blowers may already cover it |
| Custom-shape closures funded by the brand (mould paid by the customer) | INVESTIGATE | Probably the shampoo-maker case; needs a reliable local or Chinese mould route |
| Cream jars and caps | Later | Not researched |

**Key risks**
- Multinationals (Henkel, Unilever) probably buy closures through global contracts, so do not count on them early.
- The Blida and Algiers clusters may already be served by SIPEM-type molders.
- Hinge quality: hinge breakage and in-mould closing are a mould-design competence; Chinese moulds are typical.
- Closure inputs may still be imported duty-paid more cheaply than a local start-up can make them.

## 7. Next steps (to convert to VERIFIED)

1. **Interview the shampoo maker** (the founder's contact). Ask:
   - which closure type, neck finish and diameter they needed;
   - the bottle supplier, and whether the neck is standard;
   - annual volume, lot size and number of colours;
   - which local suppliers they asked and what the answer was ("no mould", "MOQ too high", "lead time", "quality");
   - what they do now (import? which country? price per 1,000? lead time?).
2. **Call SIPEM (Oued Smar) and Laboratoires SPIC (Mohammadia).** Ask about range, MOQ, lead time, price per 1,000 and capacity. If both have 24/410 and 28/410 flip-tops at low MOQ, the "gap" shrinks to custom shapes only.
3. **Call 3 bottle blowers** (Soloplast, PAP Plast, HM Plast). Ask which necks they blow, where they source caps, and whether they would co-sell.
