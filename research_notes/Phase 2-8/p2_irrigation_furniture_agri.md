# Phase 2: Product gaps in irrigation, agricultural equipment parts and furniture hardware

Research date: 2026-10-04. Data files:
- `/home/user/ghanoubenz/data/phase2_irrigation_furniture_opportunities.csv`: 55 candidates, IF-01 to IF-55. Every row has clamp, shot, fit, unit cost and margin computed.
- `/home/user/ghanoubenz/data/phase2_irrigation_furniture_signals.csv`: 25 signals.

**Method and limits (read first).**
- I ran about 40 web searches in French, Arabic and English, including Ouedkniss-restricted, Arabic-dialect and made-in-china / Alibaba queries. After that, the session's shared search cap (200) was exhausted.
- **Fetch was blocked for every domain tried:** ouedkniss.com, mabricole.com.dz, ennaharonline.com, lesenjeuxeco.dz, icex.es, andaluciatrade.es and made-in-china.com. Everything below therefore comes from search-engine snippets, plus sourced items carried over from Phase 1 (marked "(Phase 1)").
- I did not reach any Facebook page and could not open an SIPSA-Filaha exhibitor list.
- I invented no prices, buyers, suppliers or shortages. Geometry, cycle times, mold costs and selling prices in the CSV are **engineering ASSUMPTIONS**, and are labelled that way in each row's `stage_notes`.

**The bottom line in one sentence.** The research chain breaks at link 1 (pain): I found no named Algerian buyer with a documented sourcing problem in any of the three topics. What does exist are (a) structural import dependence, (b) fast-growing import-served demand in irrigation, and (c) a state OEM (AGM) with a stated plastic-part localisation need. Every family is therefore at best INVESTIGATE until a field or phone survey is done.

## 1. Who are the buyers, and how is each product family supplied today?

### Takeaway
The buyers are known only at directory level, and none has been confirmed as buying any specific part.
- **Irrigation:** the buyers are northern distributors and tape/tube makers (Mitidja, Boumerdès, Sétif–Aïn Arnat, Algiers). They serve end demand in Biskra, El Oued, Adrar and Ouargla. Imported fittings are most likely Chinese and Spanish/Italian.
- **Poultry:** equipment is imported (Italy seen once, China dominant in B2B listings).
- **Furniture:** the buyers are a handful of series makers (Divindus AMM, EACIM, SM Mobilier, Dhikra Office, MAM) plus fragmented clusters, supplied by free-entry quincaillerie importers.

## 2. What pain was found?

### Takeaway
**No named-buyer pain was found for any product in scope.** I found no shortage, MOQ, import-delay, colour, custom-size, quality-failure or lead-time complaint tied to a named Algerian buyer. The only pain evidence is structural or sector-level, and all of it is weak.

### Cited Findings
- **Irrigation demand growth (not pain).** Algerian imports of irrigation equipment (HS 8424) grew about 91.8%, and drip systems are among the main products demanded (ICEX 2025 study; snippet only). — [ICEX](https://www.icex.es/content/dam/icex/centros/argelia/documentos/2025/estudio-mercado-maquinaria-tecnologia-agricola-argelia-2025-resumen.pdf)
- **Drip subsidy.** An Ennahar headline reports a subsidy of up to 60% on drip-irrigation equipment. The search summary adds that flexible irrigation hoses are excluded, but that summary may mix in a Moroccan article. The article is undated. — [Ennahar](https://www.ennaharonline.com/دعــم-يصــل-إلى-60-علــى-أجهــزة-السقــ/)
- **Drip adoption.** Less than 25% of agricultural surfaces use drip (headline, undated). — [Algérie Eco](https://algerie-eco.com/?p=30507)
- **Poultry.** The broiler sector is chronically under-equipped and almost fully import-reliant for equipment. This is academic and undated. — [ENSV](https://depot.ensv.dz:8443/jspui/bitstream/123456789/1194/3/Probl%c3%a9matique%20de%20la%20gestion%20de%20l%e2%80%99%c3%a9levage%20du%20poulet%20de%20chair%20en%20Alg%c3%a9rie.pdf)
- **Pivot parts.** The state OEM AGM was urged to localise plastic components through a partnership with the public firm Anabib. — [Radio Algérie AR](https://news.radioalgerie.dz/ar/node/61197) (snippet; attribution uncertain)
- **Industry-wide supply.** In the ONS Q2-2025 survey, 30% of private industrial bosses said raw-material supply was below demand. — [TSA](https://www.tsa-algerie.com/industrie-algerienne-ce-que-revele-lenquete-de-lons-sur-les-entreprises/)
- **Furniture precedent.** Furniture prices surged when wood imports were put under import authorisations, renewed semi-annually; some makers withdrew from housing contracts. The source is undated and probably 2017–18. — [Algérie Eco](https://algerie-eco.com/?p=21926)
- **Phase 1 search gap.** Phase 1 web searches found no Algerian maker of drip fittings or furniture feet. This is weak evidence of a supply gap, because small workshops are poorly indexed online. — Phase 1 `import_products_longlist.md`

### Inferences
- For a press owner, the most plausible real pains are hypotheses only:
  - Drip-fitting distributors may hold seasonal stock bought months ahead (cash tied up), and may run out of specific sizes in peak season.
  - Furniture makers may not find caps in their exact tube gauge, or cam covers in their board colour, in small lots.
  - Poultry farms may face nipple leaks or stock-outs.
- The Algerian policy pattern (import licensing in 2017–18 and 2020–21, the semi-annual import programme filing since 2025, and DAPS) means **availability shocks recur**. A local maker of consumable parts is a hedge for distributors. This is an inference.

### Gaps
- No Ouedkniss, Facebook or forum content was readable, so the classic sources of complaint text were unreachable.
- No customs HS-6 data was found, so stock-outs cannot be inferred from import dips.

## 5. Machine fit and economics (from the CSV)

### Takeaway
Of the 55 candidates:
- **36 fit a 120 t press confidently.**
- **11 are borderline.** Almost all of these are borderline only because the total shot is below the 45 g practical minimum, which is fixed by adding cavities or choosing the small-screw option.
- **7 require a larger press.** These are disc filters, valve boxes, pan feeders, chick drinker bases, incubator trays, queen excluders and 5-star chair bases.
- **1 needs another process** (EPDM grommet).

Clamp force is never the constraint for the small parts (18–60 t). Shot size and mold size are.

### Cited Findings
- The reference press data and formula come from `/home/user/ghanoubenz/data/OPPORTUNITY_SCHEMA.md`. Clamp = projected area × cavities × 1.15 × factor × 1.2. Practical PP shot on a 120 t press is 45–200 g, and the tie-bar distance is 410 mm.
- Cost inputs are ASSUMPTIONS documented in each row:
  - resin PP/PE 300, POM 700, PA6 650, ABS 500, TPU 1,200 DZD/kg (PP from Phase-1 estimate of 220–350);
  - press-hour 1,400 DZD fully loaded, excluding the mold; OEE 85%;
  - molds amortised at 240 DZD/USD; 5% for packaging.
  - Phase 1 cost inputs: electricity 4.7–5.5 DZD/kWh, SNMG 24,000 DZD — [cost_inputs_market_prices.md](../Algeria%20injection%20molding%20feasibility/cost_inputs_market_prices.md)
- Chinese small multi-cavity molds cost roughly USD 4–15k, with a T1 lead time of 25–45 working days. — (Phase 1, molds_sourcing_shipping.md)

### Inferences
- **Best technical fits:** the drip tape-connector family (IF-01 to IF-08), poultry nipple body and cup (IF-28, IF-29), and furniture tube caps and glides (IF-39 to IF-42). Each is 16–32 cavities, about 50–80 g shots, with 350–380 mm molds.
- **IF-44 levelling foot shows a negative margin** at the lower-band price of 40 DZD, because the bolt and the PA6 resin cost too much. It only works at 60+ DZD.
- IF-50 (sofa leg) and IF-43 (screw-in glide) are thin at under 20% margin.

### Gaps
- All geometry needs physical samples. Mold quotes are needed for the top 5 parts.
