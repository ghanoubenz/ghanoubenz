## 1. Who is in the buying centre, per sector, and who can block a new supplier?

### Takeaway
There are two distinct buying regimes:
- **Family SMEs.** These are most joinery, furniture, irrigation-fitting and small detergent/cosmetic firms. The **owner/gérant** decides suppliers and price, and also controls the chequebook. Centralised owner decision-making is documented in the Algerian SME literature.
- **Groups and multinationals** (Henkel, Cevital/Oxxo, Condor, Cosider, SGT-type packaging firms). These have **formal purchasing functions**: job ads show "acheteur technique" and "acheteur senior" roles that analyse needs with departments, select and negotiate suppliers, and follow delivery and quality. Some use formal multi-criteria supplier scoring (AHP at Cosider Canalisations). They also expect a full administrative file (RC, NIF, NIS, CNAS/CASNOS, extrait de rôle).

Who approves samples and who can veto a new supplier is **not documented anywhere online**. It must come from interviews.

### Inferences
- **Two buyer types need two selling playbooks.**
  - **Owner-led SMEs.** One meeting with the owner can take you from introduction to price and payment terms. The risk is personal: payment discipline and whim.
  - **Groups.** Expect a sequence: purchasing file (RC/NIF/NIS/CNAS/CASNOS/extrait de rôle), then a technical/QA sample, then a trial order, then the approved list. That means 3–9 months (ASSUMPTION). The real veto usually sits with QA/R&D, not with the buyer.
- **The likely gatekeeper in SME channels is the incumbent bundler.** For bottles and caps this is the bottle trader or blower; for joinery and electrical it is the distributor. Sell *through* them, not around them. (WEAK SIGNAL / ASSUMPTION)
- **Make-vs-buy is a hidden competitor.** Venus (reportedly), Condor, Brandt and SIPLAST mold in-house. A large buyer's "no" may mean "we will make it ourselves" (WEAK SIGNAL).

### Gaps
- No evidence on who physically approves samples, how many signatures a new supplier needs, or whether purchasing in groups (Cevital, Condor) uses a vendor portal. The Béjaïa and M'sila theses could not be read.
- No evidence on the role of the *comptable* or *DAF* in family SMEs as a payment gatekeeper. Ask in the interview (question 13).

## 2. How do Algerian B2B buyers pay, and how do suppliers secure payment?

### Takeaway
- **Instruments.** B2B payment runs on cheques, transfers, bills of exchange (traite) and promissory notes. Retail and small distribution still run on cash, and in Dec 2025–Jan 2026 the Bank of Algeria tried to force cash out of commercial accounts.
- **Bounced cheques.** These are a recognised, criminally sanctioned problem: Penal Code arts. 374–375, plus a BoA prevention regime and the Centrale des impayés. Unlike Tunisia (2024–25) and Morocco (2025–26), **no decriminalisation in Algeria was found**.
- **Public buyers.** Public clients have a documented history of very long payment delays. ENIEM is the extreme case.
- **Factoring.** Factoring by banks was only regulated in **August 2026** (BoA Regulation 26-03), so it is not yet a practical tool.
- **Credit days.** No survey gives typical 30/60/90-day terms for Algerian industrial B2B: **UNKNOWN**.

### Inferences
- **Default payment terms to offer (ASSUMPTION, to validate):**
  - **New SME customers:** cash, or a certified cheque, or transfer **before or on delivery** for the first 2–3 orders. After that, 30 days by **traite avalisée** (bank-endorsed bill) or post-dated cheque for proven accounts.
  - **Groups and multinationals:** accept 60 days by transfer; they pay reliably but slowly.
  - **Public buyers (ENIEM-type):** advance payment or nothing.
- **Securing payment with what Algerian law gives:**
  - Ask for the RC and NIF, and check that the buyer's cheques are not on a ban. The Centrale des impayés is accessible only through banks, so ask your bank. ASSUMPTION
  - Prefer a **traite acceptée/avalisée** to an ordinary cheque for credit.
  - Keep invoice and delivery-note (BL) discipline, because the criminal cheque regime is the creditor's leverage.
  - Do not sell on credit to "sans facture" buyers.
- **Effect of the cash squeeze (Dec 2025–Jan 2026).** It probably **lengthened** distributors' payment cycles, because cash takes longer to clear into a bank account, and it raised their preference for cheques. Expect distributors to ask for more credit days in 2026. ASSUMPTION
- **Factoring.** Do not plan on it in 2026–27. Revisit when a bank publishes a factoring product under Reg. 26-03.

## 3. What makes an Algerian industrial buyer switch supplier, and what makes them stay?

### Takeaway
There is no Algeria-specific study of supplier switching. The strongest *behavioral* evidence is indirect, from **supply shocks**:
- Import controls (Algex prior approval since April 2022, the PPI, prior domiciliation with a 120% provision from May 2026) caused input and spare-part shortages.
- Buyers responded by integrating vertically (Venus, reportedly), developing local suppliers (Henkel's "wall-to-wall"), or using the cabas channel, which was legalised in June 2025.

Switching is triggered by **stock-outs and blocked imports**. Retention probably rests on availability, the owner relationship and fit.

### Cited Findings
- **Prior approval of imports.** Since April 2022, imports have needed **Algex** approval that the goods are not produced in sufficient quantity locally. The cereal sector reported a lack of inputs, **spare parts** and phytosanitary products as a result. — [Maghreb Émergent](https://maghrebemergent.news/fr/algerie-les-restrictions-a-limportation-commencent-a-peser-sur-lagriculture/) (snippet). **STRONG SIGNAL**
- **Spare parts became scarce.** Car spare parts grew "increasingly rare", a "parcours du combattant" for motorists. — [TSA](https://www.tsa-algerie.com/piece-de-rechange-en-algerie-parcours-du-combattant-des-automobilistes/). In 2023, licences were issued again for many products, which a hostile Moroccan outlet frames as an admission of failure. — [Le360](https://afrique.le360.ma/economie/encore-un-aveu-dechec-du-regime-algerien-apres-des-annees-dinterdiction-les-importations-a-nouveau_XVQBOEEJGBEDVLIF5MJG3I6YJM/). **STRONG SIGNAL** for shortages; note the source bias.

### Inferences
- **The strongest behavioral switch trigger in 2022–2026 is availability failure** caused by import controls, not price. Retention for a local supplier will therefore come from **holding stock** and **never stocking out** at the buyer's critical moment (STRONG SIGNAL / ASSUMPTION mix).
- **Cabas competes directly with a local molder** on small, high-value-per-kg spare parts. A local molder wins on volume, bulky parts, custom fit and an invoice (needed by formal buyers). Cabas wins on one-off, brand-name parts.
- **Expect dual sourcing.** Buyers burned by stock-outs will keep the import or cabas source as a backup and use the new local source in parallel (ASSUMPTION). Aim to be the *primary* source by service level, not to demand exclusivity early.

## 4. Who funds tooling (molds) in Algeria?

### Takeaway
**UNKNOWN for Algeria.** No Algerian source on mold-funding norms was found. The search budget ran out before the "moule client / à façon" Ouedkniss queries could run.
- **International norm:** the customer pays for and owns the mold, written into the contract, and the molder stores and maintains it. This is documented for China sourcing (prior notes).
- **Algeria hypothesis:** small owner-led buyers resist paying for molds upfront. That is one candidate explanation of the shampoo-cap gap ("converters want 100k+ or a mould paid upfront" is a Phase 1 hypothesis, not evidence).

### Cited Findings
- Chinese production tooling costs about US$5,000–15,000+ (multi-cavity) and US$1,000–3,000 for prototype molds. A common payment split is 50/40/10. The contract should state that the mold is the buyer's property, or the factory may refuse to release it. — (prior notes) [Haizol 2026](https://www.haizol.com/news/china-injection-molding-industry-report-2026); [NewBuyingAgent](https://www.newbuyingagent.com/wiki/sample-mold-fees); [ChinaImportal](https://www.chinaimportal.com/?p=4731). **VERIFIED** for the China norm.
- Mold imports: 5% customs duty, 19% VAT, plus prior domiciliation with a 120% provision. That is 3–4 months from purchase order to mold on the floor. — (prior notes) [ConformePro 84.80.71](https://conformepro.dz/resources/tarif-douanier/sous-position/84.80.710000/قوالب-للمطاط-أو-المواد-البلاستيكية-للقولبة-بالحقن-أو-بالضغط); [BoA note](https://www.bank-of-algeria.dz/stoodroa/2026/05/note-01-DGC-2026.pdf). **VERIFIED / STRONG SIGNAL**
- Local mold makers exist: ALMOULES in Sétif, CFM, SNK Plastic, MIRAF and Master Mill Precision. — (prior notes) `supply_ecosystem.md`. **STRONG SIGNAL**

### Inferences (ASSUMPTION; offer all three options and see which buyers choose)
1. **Customer-funded, customer-owned mold** (standard for custom parts and for groups): 50% at order and 50% at sample approval, with a written ownership and storage clause. Groups and multinationals will accept this. Owner-led SMEs often will not.
2. **Shared or amortised mold:** the customer pays 30–50% upfront and the rest is amortised over the first N pieces with a minimum-volume commitment. Risk: no enforceable volume commitment if the buyer disappears. Ask for a traite schedule.
3. **Molder-funded stock mold** (the molder owns it and sells the standard part to many buyers): use this for universal parts such as 24/410 and 28/410 caps, glazing packers, rebar spacers, drip fittings and furniture glides. This is where a new molder builds a catalogue. It avoids the tooling-funding fight entirely.
- Exclusivity: grant it only on customer-funded molds, and time-limit it (e.g. 12–24 months) (ASSUMPTION).

### Gaps
- What Algerian molders (SNK, MIRAF, informal Ouedkniss molders) actually charge for molds and who keeps them. Ask 2–3 local molders and mold makers directly, posing as a buyer if appropriate.

## 5. What builds trust with Algerian industrial buyers?

### Takeaway
The only *documented* trust levers are institutional:
- a complete administrative file (RC, NIF, NIS, CNAS/CASNOS, extrait de rôle);
- local-integration programmes (Henkel);
- the official discourse that subcontracting needs a "real relationship of trust".

Personal introductions ("maarifa"), the owner meeting, regional networks and sample/trial practices are **not documented online** and stay as ASSUMPTION. They are culturally plausible, but the founder must verify them.

### Cited Findings
- Officials call for a "true relationship of trust" between large contractors and local subcontractors. — [Algérie360](https://www.algerie360.com/sous-traitance-lemergence-dun-reseau-national-tributaire-des-grands-donneurs-dordre/). **STRONG SIGNAL**
- Public-company managers prefer foreign subcontractors over national ones ("frilosité"). — [Algérie Eco 2017](https://algerie-eco.com/2017/03/26/entreprises-publiques-ne-appel-aux-traitants-nationaux/). **STRONG SIGNAL** of distrust of local suppliers among public buyers.
- Standard supplier file: RC, NIF, NIS, extrait de rôle, CNAS, CASNOS. — (prior notes) [Safqatic](https://safqatic.dz/docs/offres/at__N___04_2022_20220207092648.pdf). **VERIFIED**
- Fairs serve as meeting points: Batimatec, SIPSA-Filaha, ALGEST/SANIST (BASTP/CACI). — (prior notes). **VERIFIED**
- Perception of "Made in Algeria" quality: the searches returned only food-product consumer studies (dairy in Souk Ahras, olive oil), not industrial buyers. — [AgEcon](https://ageconsearch.umn.edu/record/334881/files/economierurale-4994.pdf); [HAL INRAE](https://hal.inrae.fr/hal-04875964/file/Vol_4032_Perception-Consommateurs-Algerie.pdf). **UNKNOWN** for industrial parts.

### Trust builders to test (ASSUMPTION; rank them from interview answers)
1. **Show the machine and the part.** Bring physical samples, plus a photo or video of the press and molds. Owner-led buyers buy from people and factories they have seen.
2. **Introduction through someone the buyer knows** (maarifa): a distributor, the resin supplier, a mold maker, or a fellow manufacturer in the same zone.
3. **A complete, clean administrative file** handed over at the first meeting (RC/NIF/NIS/CNAS/CASNOS). This signals a formal, invoicing supplier. It matters for groups and for buyers who must deduct VAT.
4. **Free first samples made on the real mold**, measured against the buyer's own part, then a paid trial order.
5. **Local stock and a delivery promise in days**, not weeks, against the import's 3–4 months.
6. **Price stability in DZD**, with a stated validity period, given the gap between official and parallel FX.
7. **References:** the first known customer is worth more than any certificate. ISO 9001 matters for multinationals only (ASSUMPTION).

### Gaps
- No evidence was found on regional or family network effects, on how much "Made in Algeria" distrust exists for plastic parts, or on typical trial-order sizes.

## 7. The 10-minute behavioral buyer interview (EN / FR / AR-darja)

### Design rules (improvements on the founder's draft)
- **Ask about the last real event, not opinions.** "The last time…", "show me…". Never ask "would you buy?" Opinions about future purchases are unreliable. Past behavior is the evidence.
- **Ask to see the physical part and an invoice or delivery note if possible.** It verifies price, origin and quantity.
- **Map the buying centre with names.** Ask "Who else was involved? Who signed?", not "who decides?".
- **Close with a concrete next step.** The buyer gives a sample part, a drawing, or a quantity for a quote. A buyer who will not give a part is not a lead.
- **Interview the right people.** Interview the owner/gérant in SMEs. In groups, interview the buyer *and*, separately, the QA or production person.
- **Record the answers for the database:** sector, wilaya, role, part, origin, unit price DZD, MOQ, lead time, stock-out (Y/N), last disappointment, payment terms, mold funding, decision-maker, next step.

### English version (target 10 minutes; questions 1–8 are the core, 9–16 if time allows)
1. **Last imported part.** "What is the last plastic part or component you bought from abroad, or from an importer? Can you show it to me?"
2. **Where and from whom.** "Which country, and which supplier or trader? Direct import, importer, distributor, or someone bringing it in a suitcase (cabas)?"
3. **How many, how often.** "How many pieces did you buy last time, and how often do you reorder?"
4. **Cost.** "What did it cost per piece, delivered to your door? (Do you have the invoice?) Did you pay in dinars or in foreign currency?"
5. **Delivery days and MOQ.** "How many days from order to having it in your hands? What was the minimum quantity they forced you to take?"
6. **Out of stock.** "What happened the last time it ran out? How many days were you stopped or slowed, and what did you do instead?"
7. **Hardest-to-source part.** "Which plastic part is the hardest for you to find today? Why: quantity, size, colour, quality, import paperwork?"
8. **Wished-local part.** "If one part could be made 30 km from you tomorrow, which one would it be?"
9. **Recent disappointment.** "Tell me about the last time a supplier disappointed you. What happened, and what did you do: warn, reduce orders, or switch?"
10. **Sample approval to order.** "If my sample passes your test, what happens next? Who tests it, how long does it take, and what would make you still not order?"
11. **First test order.** "What size would your first trial order be, in pieces or in dinars?"
12. **Who approves suppliers.** "For your current supplier, who found them, who checked the quality, who agreed the price, and who signed the payment? Who could say no to a new supplier?"
13. **Payment.** "How do you normally pay suppliers like this: cash, cheque, transfer, traite? After how many days? What do you need from a new supplier: RC, NIF, CNAS…?"
14. **Who pays the mold.** "If a part needs a new mold, who usually pays for it, you or the supplier? Who owns it after?"
15. **Exclusivity.** "If you pay for the mold, do you need us not to sell the part to anyone else? For how long?"
16. **Close.** "Can I take one sample (or a drawing) and come back with a price and a delivery time? Who should I send it to?"

### Version française (10 minutes)
1. **Dernière pièce importée.** « Quelle est la dernière pièce ou le dernier composant en plastique que vous avez acheté à l'étranger ou chez un importateur ? Je peux la voir ? »
2. **Où et chez qui.** « De quel pays, et chez quel fournisseur ou revendeur ? En import direct, via un importateur, un distributeur, ou par cabas ? »
3. **Combien, à quelle fréquence.** « Combien de pièces la dernière fois, et tous les combien vous recommandez ? »
4. **Coût.** « Combien vous a coûté la pièce, rendue chez vous ? (Vous avez la facture ?) Payé en dinars ou en devises ? »
5. **Délai et minimum.** « Combien de jours entre la commande et la réception ? Quelle quantité minimum on vous a imposée ? »
6. **Rupture.** « La dernière fois que vous étiez en rupture, que s'est-il passé ? Combien de jours d'arrêt ou de ralentissement, et comment avez-vous fait ? »
7. **Pièce la plus difficile.** « Quelle pièce plastique est la plus difficile à trouver aujourd'hui ? Pourquoi : quantité, dimension, couleur, qualité, papiers d'import ? »
8. **Pièce souhaitée en local.** « Si demain une seule pièce pouvait être fabriquée à 30 km d'ici, ce serait laquelle ? »
9. **Déception récente.** « Racontez-moi la dernière fois qu'un fournisseur vous a déçu. Qu'est-ce qui s'est passé, et qu'avez-vous fait : avertissement, moins de commandes, changement ? »
10. **Après l'échantillon.** « Si mon échantillon est validé, que se passe-t-il ensuite ? Qui le teste, combien de temps ça prend, et qu'est-ce qui pourrait quand même bloquer la commande ? »
11. **Première commande test.** « Quelle serait la taille de votre première commande d'essai, en pièces ou en dinars ? »
12. **Qui valide.** « Pour votre fournisseur actuel : qui l'a trouvé, qui a contrôlé la qualité, qui a négocié le prix, qui a signé le paiement ? Qui pourrait refuser un nouveau fournisseur ? »
13. **Paiement.** « Comment payez-vous ce type de fournisseur : espèces, chèque, virement, traite ? À combien de jours ? Quels documents demandez-vous à un nouveau fournisseur : RC, NIF, CNAS… ? »
14. **Qui paie le moule.** « Quand une pièce demande un nouveau moule, qui le paie d'habitude : vous ou le fournisseur ? À qui appartient-il ensuite ? »
15. **Exclusivité.** « Si vous payez le moule, voulez-vous l'exclusivité sur la pièce ? Pour combien de temps ? »
16. **Clôture.** « Je peux prendre un échantillon (ou un plan) et revenir avec un prix et un délai ? À qui je l'envoie ? »

### النسخة العربية (بالدارجة، 10 دقائق)
1. **آخر قطعة جات من برّا:** "واش هي آخر قطعة بلاستيك (ولا pièce) شريتوها من برّا ولا من عند importateur؟ نقدر نشوفها؟"
2. **منين وعند شكون:** "من أنا بلاد، وعند شكون؟ import direct، importateur، distributeur، ولا جابوها في الكابة (cabas)؟"
3. **قدّاش وكل قدّاش:** "قدّاش من حبّة شريتو آخر مرة؟ وكل قدّاش تعاودو تطلبو؟"
4. **السومة:** "بقدّاش وصلتكم الحبّة حتى للمصنع؟ (عندكم la facture؟) خلصتو بالدينار ولا بالدوفيز؟"
5. **المدة والكمية الدنيا:** "قدّاش من يوم من la commande حتى وصلتكم؟ وشحال هي أقل كمية فرضوها عليكم (minimum)؟"
6. **كي تخلاص السلعة:** "آخر مرة خلاصت عليكم (rupture)، واش صرا؟ قدّاش من يوم حبستو ولا نقصتو الخدمة، وكيفاش دبّرتو راسكم؟"

8. **القطعة اللي تحبوها تتصنع هنا:** "لو كان غدوة قطعة وحدة برك تتصنع على بعد 30 كلم منكم، واش تكون؟"

10. **بعد ما يتقبل l'échantillon:** "إذا l'échantillon تاعي نجح، واش يصرا من بعد؟ شكون يجربو، قدّاش ياخذ وقت، وواش اللي يقدر يحبس la commande؟"
11. **أول طلبية تجريبية:** "قدّاش تكون أول commande d'essai، بالحبّات ولا بالدينار؟"
12. **شكون يوافق:** "الـfournisseur اللي عندكم دُرك: شكون لقاه، شكون شاف الجودة، شكون تفاهم على السومة، شكون سيني الخلاص؟ وشكون يقدر يقول لا لـfournisseur جديد؟"
13. **الخلاص:** "كيفاش تخلصو هاد النوع من الموردين: cash، chèque، virement، traite؟ بعد قدّاش من يوم؟ واش تطلبو من fournisseur جديد: RC، NIF، CNAS…؟"
14. **شكون يخلص القالب (le moule):** "كي تحتاج قطعة moule جديد، شكون يخلصو عادةً، نتوما ولا الـfournisseur؟ ومن بعد لمن يولي؟"
15. **الحصرية (exclusivité):** "إذا خلصتو le moule، تحبو ما نبيعوش هاد القطعة لحتى واحد آخر؟ لقدّاش من وقت؟"
16. **الخاتمة:** "نقدر نديّ échantillon (ولا plan) ونرجعلكم بالسومة والمدة؟ لمن نبعثهولو؟"

### Scoring each interview (for the opportunity database)
- **Hot lead:** they hand over a part or drawing, give a unit price and annual quantity, report a stock-out in the last 12 months, and name the payer/approver.
- **Warm lead:** pain is described, but there is no part or quantity yet.
- **Cold lead:** opinions only.

Record "who can veto" and "how they pay" for every interview. These two fields fill the biggest evidence gaps in this note.
